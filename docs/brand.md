# 1. Choose the brand

<DocReview id="BRAND" />

A white T-shirt is familiar. Our brand gives people a reason to choose ours. We need to decide who it is for and what it means to wear it.

## Pick a direction

An **archetype** is a familiar personality that helps a brand feel consistent.

| Direction | The idea | Possible message |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Creator** | The shirt is simple; the person wearing it brings the personality. | “You bring the story.” |
| **Explorer** | One dependable shirt for new places and experiences. | “Go your own way.” |
| **Everyperson** | An everyday shirt for people who want to feel comfortable being themselves. | “Nothing to prove.” |

These are options, not final decisions. “White Shirt Project” is a temporary name.

## What would this look like?

If we choose Creator, we could photograph different people making things while wearing the same shirt. The colors, words, and pictures should all support that idea.

Before choosing fonts and colors, we need a brand name, a main audience, and one clear promise. We also need to agree on a sample price and shirt details; none are confirmed yet.

## Give people reasons to trust us

We can use Cialdini’s persuasion ideas in straightforward ways:

- **Give something useful:** offer a free guide to styling a white shirt. This is reciprocity.
- **Show relatable people:** tell honest stories visitors connect with. This supports liking and unity.
- **Explain the product:** show fabric, fit, and construction details we can support. This builds authority.
- **Show real feedback when we have it:** this is social proof. Any example reviews must be clearly labeled.

We can leave scarcity and other techniques out unless there is a real reason to use them. No made-up deadlines or stock counts.

## Discuss these three questions

1. Who should want this shirt?
2. Which direction do you prefer: Creator, Explorer, or Everyperson? Why?
3. What name and short message would fit that choice?
