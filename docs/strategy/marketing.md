# Content and persuasion

## Content plan proposal
Home introduces the promise. The Shirt answers product questions. Our Story explains the focus. The Lookbook shows the shirt in context. Guides address specific styling, fit, and care questions.

## Persuasion approach
| Principle | Proposed application | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reciprocity | Useful styling guide without an email gate | Original, useful content |
| Commitment and consistency | Optional styling preferences | A clear benefit and easy reset |
| Social proof | Real wearer feedback | Genuine experience and permission |
| Authority | Explain fabric, fit, and construction | Verified specifications or testing |
| Liking | Human stories and approachable voice | Accurate biographies and image rights |
| Scarcity | Explain actual production limits if any | Real inventory or deadlines |
| Unity | Shared values expressed through wearer stories | A defined audience and brand direction |

Use [Cialdini’s framework](https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/) as a design lens. No fabricated reviews, stock counts, endorsements, or urgency. Illustrative content must be labeled.

## Discoverability proposal
Publish substantive HTML pages with descriptive titles, descriptions, headings, image alternatives, and internal links. Plan social preview images, a sitemap, and Search Console setup. Keep checkout and cart out of the search sitemap and mark them noindex. Keep project documentation distinct from consumer content; documentation pages currently use noindex.

## Content inventory
For each future asset record its page, owner, source, rights, alt text, claim evidence, and approval status. No product photos, customer quotes, or material claims are verified yet.
